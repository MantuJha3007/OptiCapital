# Empirical Crisis Research & Correlation Breakdown

## The Illusion of Diversification in Crises
Financial market history (1987 Black Monday, 1998 LTCM, 2008 Global Financial Crisis, 2020 COVID Liquidity Freeze) proves that nominal portfolio diversification consistently collapses during systemic stress:

### Correlation Contagion
During liquidity contractions, institutional market participants are forced to sell liquid risk assets across asset classes to satisfy margin calls. As a result:
- Pairwise correlations between Equities, Corporate Bonds, and Emerging Market assets spike from a normal range of 0.35 - 0.45 to over 0.80 - 0.85.
- Diversification benefits vanish precisely when downside protection is most critical.
- A nominal multi-asset portfolio collapses into a single high-beta risk cluster.

### Minimum-Intervention Defense Strategy
Chasing theoretical "optimal" unconstrained portfolios during severe crises causes massive turnover, high slippage, and whipsaw losses. Empirical institutional research demonstrates that the superior defensive protocol is **Minimum Intervention**:
1. Execute the smallest feasible reallocation necessary to bring capital back inside the Safe Operating Envelope.
2. Maintain high cash liquidity (minimum 15% - 20%) to absorb further volatility shocks.
3. Protect the Distance to Failure (DtF) by ensuring that even an additional 15% shock cannot breach capital preservation boundaries.
